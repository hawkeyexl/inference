A short page about a decision you will make in your first week and then repeatedly.

## The principle

> **The library owns what is identical across consumers. You own what is specific to your domain.**

Everything below follows from that, and the principle is more useful than the list — the list will
always be incomplete.

## The split

| The library owns | You own |
|---|---|
| Talking to a provider | Your prompts, and their versioning |
| Validating a response, and the retry | Your schema, and its wording |
| Counting votes, computing agreement | What a verdict means in your domain |
| Zone routing | What each zone does in your product |
| The cache **mechanism** | What goes in the **key** |
| Pricing tokens | Your budget policy |
| One subject at a time | Concurrency, retries, batching |
| A flat `ProviderSpec` | Mapping your config onto it |

## Why this page exists

All three production consumers independently wrote a version of the same rule into their own
contributor instructions:

> Never reimplement a provider, ensemble, cache, or price table here; three copies of that code
> drifted apart once already, and a fix belongs upstream.

That sentence exists three times, in three repositories, **because the library never said it**.

It is not a hypothetical. This package exists *because* the same code had been copied into three
projects and drifted — each copy holding a fix the others lacked. One had the Windows argv limit
solved, another had the non-throwing cache write, a third had the OpenAI strict-mode fallback. The
extraction was the fix. The boundary is the whole reason the package exists.

<Aside type="danger" title="The specific anti-pattern">
  If you find yourself writing a provider adapter, an ensemble loop, a cache implementation, or a
  price table **inside your own repository**, stop. That is how the fourth divergent copy starts,
  and nobody notices until a fix lands in one of them.
</Aside>

## What to do instead

**If the fix is in a provider, the ensemble, the cache, or the price table** — it belongs upstream.
Open an issue or a PR against this package. Every consumer gets it, including the three that already
exist.

**If you need behavior the contract does not offer** — say so upstream before working around it. The
provider contract is deliberately narrow, and widening it
[requires an ADR](https://github.com/hawkeyexl/inference/tree/main/adrs), but "deliberately narrow"
is not the same as "closed."

**If it is domain-specific** — it is yours, and the library will not try to take it. There is no
`PROMPT_VERSION` here, no domain prompt text, no opinion about what invalidates your cache entries.
Those absences are deliberate.

## The four you definitely own

Worth naming, because each is somewhere a reader expects the library to help and it deliberately
does not:

1. **Prompts and their versioning.** Ship no prompt text, hold no `PROMPT_VERSION`. See
   [caching](/inference/judge/caching/#you-own-the-key).
2. **Verdict wording.** The structure is the library's; the field descriptions are yours, and they
   are [prompt surface](/inference/judge/verdict-schema/).
3. **Orchestration.** Concurrency, backoff, per-subject status. See
   [running at scale](/inference/judge/at-scale/).
4. **Config mapping.** `ProviderSpec` is flat and library-owned so it never has to know about your
   config format. Map onto it; do not pass your config object through. See
   [choosing a provider](/inference/get-started/choose-a-provider/#the-spec).
